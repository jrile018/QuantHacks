# How a Lattice state could become a trading decision: primary-source review

Date: 2026-10-03. Scope: research and an implementation specification, **not** a trading result. This note uses original papers, author-hosted copies, and exchange material. Historical results below belong to their authors' samples and policies; none is a forecast for this project.

## Decision for this project

Implement one **equity residual-state forecast** first: remove a small, tradable market/sector factor set from daily stock returns, accumulate the residual, fit a past-only AR(1) reversion model, and forecast the next-session residual return. Evaluate the forecast on identical rows against zero, ticker mean, ordinary own-history/reversal, market/sector factors, and a simple sector-peer residual. Make Lattice graph-neighbor residuals one *registered challenger* to those controls. A graph earns inclusion only if it improves next-session prediction on genuinely new data and later improves a separately frozen, executable long-stock/short-factor or reverse policy after costs. No previous study here qualifies a live trade.

This choice is closest to [Avellaneda and Lee's original equity strategy](https://math.nyu.edu/faculty/avellane/AvellanedaLeeStatArb071108.pdf), but preserves the project's accepted daily/next-session target and its real information clocks. The source's 60-day window and cutoffs are candidate **mechanism references**, not parameters already validated in 2024–2025. The paper trades a portfolio over a succession of days, so a one-session forecast is a narrower statistical question than replicating its full strategy.

Current evidence: the project's [15 fixed contextual comparisons](../../2026-10-03-contextual-lattice-results.md) found context plus Lattice lowest squared error in **0/15** cells. Equity absolute movement was lowest for Lattice alone, but did not establish direction or option profit. Futures had only 60–61 training rows per root. The available equity adjusted-close panel can support *statistical* returns; its vintage, source receipt, and executable close are not certified. Business-link/expectation/publication-clock rows are unqualified; the primary options audit has 18 events. These facts prohibit relabeling this research as an already tested economic-link, futures, options, or profit result.

## What the components do

| Component | Defensible role | It does **not** establish |
|---|---|---|
| Correlation distance, kNN or MST | Past-data peer/candidate selection or regime summary. [Mantegna's original paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/cond-mat/9802256) obtains an economically interpretable stock hierarchy from synchronous return correlations. | A directional edge, stationary spread, causal business link, or profitable entry rule. MST forces connectivity even when its weakest edge is bad. |
| PCA | Estimate shared return factors, then isolate a residual to test; in Avellaneda–Lee, factors are eigenportfolio returns built from standardized daily returns. | A guaranteed tradeable hedge, especially if factor weights are unconstrained or change at rebalance. |
| Rotationally invariant/nonlinear eigenvalue shrinkage (RIE) | Stabilize covariance/eigenvalue estimates when names are numerous relative to dates. [Ledoit–Wolf's original estimator](https://www.ledoit.net/Analytical_AoS_2020.pdf) is a covariance-estimation method. | Signal alpha. Better matrix estimation must be tested on downstream hedge risk and turnover. |
| Residual state and AR(1)/OU | Determine whether a *signed, factor-adjusted* excursion predicts next-period residual change. | A causal reason for reversion or a net profitable trade. The estimated residual must actually mean revert at a useful speed. |
| Dated business graph | Directed exposure from a known customer to supplier, with timing and economic sign; see [Cohen–Frazzini](https://pages.stern.nyu.edu/~afrazzin/pdf/Economic%20Links%20and%20Predictable%20Returns%20-%20Cohen%20and%20Frazzini.pdf). | Equivalence to a correlation graph or a generic next-day effect. It needs historical link identity, publication time, and prior expectation. |

### Two source details that change design

**Distance is not correlation.** Gatev selects by squared distance of *normalized cumulative total-return paths* and measures a spread excursion from that formation distribution. Lattice's correlation distance `sqrt(2(1−rho))` is about synchronous *daily returns*. Two high-correlation names can have a drifting relative price because their betas/drifts differ. Gatev explicitly warns that apparently related prices may be spuriously correlated. If Lattice graph peers are used as a candidate screen, calculate and validate an actual hedge-weighted spread and its out-of-window reversion separately. Do not call it cointegrated because an MST contains an edge.

**Residual prediction differs from stock prediction.** In Avellaneda–Lee, stock return is split into factor component plus residual, and a hypothetical $1 long stock position is paired with short beta dollars of factor ETF/eigenportfolio. The expected conditional residual change has sign `m−x`; the actual stock direction additionally depends on factor moves and drift. A residual-reversal score may improve *hedged spread* expectation while losing stock next-close forecast. Report both, with their units and scoring rules.

### Endpoint pinning in Avellaneda–Lee: what they actually do

In the [author PDF, Appendix §9, equations (20)–(22) and footnote 10](https://math.nyu.edu/faculty/avellane/AvellanedaLeeStatArb071108.pdf), equation (20) regresses the 60 stock returns on an intercept and ETF returns, `R^S_n = β₀ + βR^I_n + ε_n`. They define `X_k = Σ_{j=1}^k ε_j`. OLS with an intercept imposes `Σ_{j=1}^{60} ε_j = 0`, hence `X₆₀ = 0` **by construction**. They state this is an artifact of estimating betas and residuals from the same observations. Their equation (21) fits `X_{n+1}=a+bX_n+ζ_{n+1}` and maps `b=e^(−κΔt)`, `m=a/(1−b)`, with equilibrium standard deviation from the AR innovation variance. Because `X₆₀=0`, equation (22) evaluates the score as `s=(0−m)/σ_eq` (then centers `m` across stocks). That score is driven by the fitted mean relative to an artificially pinned endpoint; the zero endpoint is **not evidence that prices converged**. The paper screens out `b` near one using `κ>252/30`, but this filter cannot undo the OLS identity.

Footnote 10 proposes estimating the return regression over **90 days** and the residual process over **60 days**. That makes the 60-day subset's residual sum generally nonzero, though the footnote does not specify a strict disjoint split or demonstrate a revised performance result. The paper's main documented procedure uses the 60-day joint fit and explicitly simplifies the score with `X₆₀=0`. Its backtest looks backward at each decision and therefore need not use *future* information to suffer this problem. The concern is **in-sample geometry and biased stationarity/score selection**, rather than temporal look-ahead by itself. We should not describe their reported Sharpe ratios as evidence that a disjoint-window version works.

**Pilot correction, chosen for this project rather than claimed as their method:** at close of decision date `t`, use three chronological, nonoverlapping blocks per security. Estimate `(α_A,β_A)` on 252 sessions `A=[t−312,t−61]` using stock and one predeclared sector ETF (or the same small factor set for every comparator). Freeze those coefficients. On the next 60 sessions `B=[t−60,t−1]`, compute *unrefitted* residual returns `e_j=r^S_j−α_A−β_A r^I_j` and cumulative `X_k=Σ_{j=1}^k e_j`, starting at zero on the first B boundary. Fit `X_{k+1}=a_B+φ_B X_k+u_{k+1}` only on transitions contained in B. At the newly completed close `t`, score an observation held out of both coefficient fits: `e_t=r^S_t−α_A−β_A r^I_t`, `X_t=X_{60}+e_t`, and `s_t=(X_t−m_B)/σ_{eq,B}`, where `m_B=a_B/(1−φ_B)` and `σ_{eq,B}=sd(u_B)/sqrt(1−φ_B²)` for `0<φ_B<1`. The next-session *factor-adjusted* return forecast is `α_A+a_B+(φ_B−1)X_t`; its realized hedged cash return before costs is `r^S_{t+1}−β_A r^I_{t+1}`, which includes `α_A` and requires actual hedge legs for an economic test. Do not use the later `t+1` factor return as a decision-time stock forecast: add a separately frozen factor-return forecast for a stock-return target, or report the hedged-return forecast as a separate target.

The proposed 252/60/1 lengths are a predeclared design choice, **not** from Avellaneda–Lee. Each rolling decision recalibrates from information known by `t`; within a decision, keep `α_A,β_A` fixed across B, `t`, and the `t+1` outcome. A daily overlapping sequence of decisions needs date/security-clustered evaluation and a genuinely later untouched interval. Log `X_{60}` and `X_t` distributions; a nearly universal zero endpoint indicates recentering or refitting has recreated the artifact. An arbitrary state origin does not itself prove stationarity: the decisive tests are residual autocorrelation/half-life stability and **out-of-sample** conditional prediction versus a no-reversion random-walk/own-residual baseline. Reject a fitted `φ_B` outside `(0,1)` or an impractically long half-life; predeclare how such rows abstain. Only promote this state if it beats the simpler baselines on matched next-session rows and, later, a costed, achievable hedge policy.

## Narrow, falsifiable implementation contract

1. **Universe and clocks.** Choose a predeclared liquid equity cohort with historical identities, delistings, corporate actions, and both stock and broad/sector ETF observations. At a completed session close *t*, use only data whose observation/retrieval basis is available for that decision. The outcome remains session *t+1* adjusted-close return for the statistical comparison. Label it a research mark; a trade simulation must start at the first demonstrably executable price after signal availability, mark an actual stock and hedge leg, and pay both sides' spreads/fees/borrow/financing.
2. **Factor-first baseline.** Fit past-only stock betas on a small market plus sector ETF set; use point-in-time ETF availability and memberships. A single sector ETF is a defensible minimal analogue to Avellaneda–Lee. With the current small cohort, a 15-factor PCA is rank/overfit-prone and should remain a separately registered comparator if enough independent names and history exist. Record regression fit window, intercept, residual-return units (fractional return), beta sign, and hedge dollars. Exclude the target from a synthetic peer factor or explicitly account for self-inclusion.
3. **State model.** Use the separated `A/B/t` fit and score above. A valid OU mapping needs `0<φ_B<1`, `κ=−ln(φ_B)` per session, `τ=1/κ`, finite innovation variance, and stable enough parameters. Report both the signed next-session hedged-return forecast and the score. The paper's 60-session fit/≤30-session reversion gate is a reference specification, but a gate fitted to 60 noisy transitions can select apparent reversion; test an unfiltered/abstention-aware control. Also test plain own-residual AR(1)/reversal so OU notation receives no credit. Log failures and abstentions when fit, half-life, or variance is invalid; never clip an invalid fit into a profitable-looking score.
4. **Graph challenger.** Freeze one Lattice-style graph estimator/window and choose neighbors using **only preceding eligible returns**, preferably after the same factor removal. A neighbor basket may have signed hedge coefficients estimated from past returns; the current nonnegative basket cannot represent arbitrary signed factor exposures. Build the hedge-weighted residual state with a frozen construction rule. Compare **identical-capacity** simple sector peers, top absolute-correlation peers, normalized-price-distance pairs, and the Lattice kNN/MST selection, all with the same state/forecast fit and rows. Remove graph-specific inputs and retest; the graph is useful only for an incremental gain after the factor and peer controls. RIE is optional only if covariance instability is diagnosed and shrinkage beats a simple linear-shrinkage baseline on a prespecified downstream metric.
5. **Evaluation and policy gate.** Freeze rolling fit dates and a later untouched/prospective evaluation interval; 2024/2025 has already been inspected in this project. Use next-session directional/residual forecast loss against zero/mean/factors; report matched opportunity count, sign, uncertainty clustered by date and security, and all model-selection trials. For a later policy, predeclare entry/exit timing, s-score cutoffs or decision threshold, maximum gross/net exposure, stock borrow eligibility, factor-hedge rebalancing, and conservative bid/ask/fee/borrow assumptions. Compare long/short hedged cash P&L against an equal-exposure factor-only/residual rule and a simple price-distance pair rule. Record forced liquidations and no-trades. **Forecast improvement is necessary evidence, not itself a profit claim.**

Potential regression failure modes: recalculating a 60-day state with full-sample betas; choosing the graph or factor count after seeing outcomes; selecting only surviving/current constituents; using revised adjusted prices without a vintage; treating an end-of-day signal as fillable at that same close; mis-signing a hedge; treating 20 pairs or 20 option strikes on one date as independent observations; comparing a larger graph model with an underpowered baseline; and reusing the inspected 2024/2025 periods as a final test. Gatev's one-day delay sensitivity is direct evidence that the fill boundary matters.

## Adjacent markets: role and deferral

- **Equity options:** An equity residual forecast can condition an underlying-direction scenario; it cannot by itself price a call, put, or straddle. The option decision additionally needs synchronized contract bid/ask, moneyness, expiry, rates/dividends, exercise/adjustment terms, implied-volatility surface and Greeks, and a quote-valid entry/exit rule. Common option-return factors are a meaningful null; [Goyal–Saretto's published paper](https://academic.oup.com/rfs/article/38/6/1783/8010873) studies monthly delta-hedged strategies, a different target. The current 18-event primary audit cannot validate an option policy.
- **Futures:** A stock residual may suggest a *beta hedge* with ES/MES only for a known equity holding, sized in actual contract notional ([CME](https://www.cmegroup.com/education/whitepapers/hedging-with-e-mini-sp-500-future)). ES/MES is one S&P exposure, not two independent alpha samples. ZN, CL and GC need their own curve, rates, inventory or cash-exposure hypotheses; copying an equity correlation graph does not supply a directional reason. Current same-contract risk-unit outcomes are not percentage equity returns; only 60–61 qualified training rows are available per root. A futures hedge test requires a fixed underlying portfolio, dated beta/DV01/commodity exposure, fees, margin and liquidation ledger.
- **Economic links and event delays:** Cohen–Frazzini supports a future *directed* customer-shock-to-supplier study once dated named links, effective/public times and expectations exist. Its monthly horizon and broad historical universe do not license a daily next-session causal interpretation. A same-day customer close cannot enter a decision that occurred before that close. Test the signed source shock, own/industry shock and ordinary sector lead–lag controls before graph reliability interactions.
- **Longer horizons:** Gatev holds up to six months; Avellaneda–Lee's typical reversion time is several days; Cohen–Frazzini sorts monthly. The user has now approved **5- and 20-session secondary horizons** alongside the primary daily/next-session target. Register their targets, overlap-aware evaluation and trial family separately. Their results cannot replace a failed next-session result or borrow its economic interpretation.

## Source-quality and inference notes

The Yale copy is the authors' 2006 revision (1962–2002); the earlier [NBER 1999 abstract](https://www.nber.org/papers/w7032) covers 1962–1997 and quotes “up to 12%.” Use the 2006 numbers with the 2006 sample, not mixed with the 1999 date. The Avellaneda–Lee PDF is author-hosted and gives simulated, assumed-cost Sharpe ratios, not verified current net returns. Cohen–Frazzini's result is an original published historical monthly portfolio; its “net effect” in a regression section means *after hedging explanatory factors*, not necessarily *after trading costs*. Mantegna and Ledoit–Wolf establish mathematical representation/estimation, not strategy profitability. The local experimental findings are project evidence rather than independent replication of any paper.
